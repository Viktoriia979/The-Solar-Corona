The Solar Corona

The solar corona is the outermost layer of the Sun’s atmosphere. It is made up of extremely hot gases called plasma and can reach temperatures of around 1 million degrees Celsius or more. The corona extends millions of kilometres into space and is much less dense than the layers underneath it.

The solar corona formed as part of the Sun when it developed about 4.6 billion years ago. It is heated by energy from inside the Sun, especially through the movement and activity of its powerful magnetic fields. Scientists are still studying exactly how the corona becomes so much hotter than the Sun’s surface.

The solar corona is difficult to see normally because the bright surface of the Sun is much brighter than it. We can see the corona during a total solar eclipse, when the Moon blocks the Sun’s bright surface and the corona becomes visible as a white, glowing halo around it. Scientists can also study the corona using special instruments called coronagraphs and spacecraft that observe the Sun.
